Where you startedWhere you finished

Selection-bias reporting lives in three places.

Methods: who was sampled and invited, and how the authors checked for and addressed bias.

Results: how many people were lost at each stage, and how the participants compare with those who were invited.

Discussion: what the design prevented, what it could not, and what that means for the findings.

Congratulations, you’ve completed the activity!

You took a manuscript that no reader could assess and rebuilt the reporting that lets them judge its selection bias.

Selection bias is rarely reported in a single sentence. It lives in who you sampled, who you lost along the way, how they compare, and what you say about it.

The next time you read a paper, or write one, look for all three: complete reporting, mitigation, and discussion.
